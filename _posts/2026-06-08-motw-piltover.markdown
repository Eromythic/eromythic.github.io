**Monster of the Week ♥ 4-6 players**

*A horrific murder has taken place in the commercial district of Piltover in broad daylight, and it will be up to you to investigate. The game will run in Monster of the Week, a Powered by the Apocalypse game of investigation and action set in Runeterra. No previous knowledge is needed.*

### Is this for you? ###

This is a Monster of the Week adventure meant for:
+ Flexible player count (4 to 6 player characters)
+ Clue-finding and mystery-solving aficionados
+ Collaborative storytelling and roleplay
+ Lightweight rules (low-simulationism)
+ New players
+ Fans of League of Legends and/or Arcane.

### Running the Session ###

**Before starting**, I recommend taking a look at the **Use Magic** move and thinking ahead of time how you want to deal with it in your game. It's easy to make it feel too much of a cheat, so you might want to consider some constraints for it before getting started and let the players know. It's also easy to commit the opposite mistake, where players feel like they're not "entitled" to Use Magic moves because their character isn't a Wizard. Think ahead of time how you will let them know that this isn't necessarily the case: borrowing strange hextech gizmos that resolve the situation is a valid "Use Magic", as well as anything else. Magic isn't limited to sorcery but basically any extraordinary things like hextech, ki, etc. Help players feel included in describing their own brands of magic.

**At the start of the session**, the players can go through the playbooks and decide amongst themselves which playbooks will be played. Then, we'll go through the party creation process as outlined in the Monster of the Week rules, except we'll only go once around the table for each character to establish one relationship with another character, to provide some context on how the characters relate to each-other without spending too much time on it. There are some additional changes regarding Luck, experience, etc. but these are already part of the Playbook and need not be addressed separately.

**Once everyone has picked their character**, the game can start normally following the script. Monster of the Week is a very narrative framework, so even though there's a Script to help guide the story somewhat, there will be lots of improvisation.

### [SPOILERS] What my session looked like ###

**As means to help illustrate** what this game might look like, as well as possibly provide some grounded examples regarding the script, I will go over what my session looked like in a summarized fashion. Our session was planned as a whole-day thing, so it ran as long as 8 hours. **You will probably want to have it be shorter** and so some intermediate fights and scenes can be removed wholesale.

**The backbone of the story is**: a criminal kingpin learns that a fairy will be stealing an object from the Ferros family's safe tonight, and they hire a crazy assassin to kill her. Fairies are very hard to kill, so a big setup is required. The unhinged assassin will perform gratuitous murders prior to their hired kill. Now, the players won't know any of this and will slowly piece it together from different clues they find.

Before even getting to **the opening scene**, some players will go through their "at the beginning of the mystery" moves and receive starting clues and information. Once this is done, the game opens with the scene of the murder of the assassin's first victim. Players investigate the crime scene and combine what they find with clues already in their possession. After this, they decide to split up and follow different investigation threads: one group goes check the police files, another group checks the docks; each gets a police-issued walkie-talkie. 

The group at the docks gets a **bad Investigate a Mystery roll**, which resolves as "the enemy learns about you". They realize their investigation is drawing heat and decide to hole up in a bar and contact their other half, who arrives rolling up in an armored SUV at the right time to turn the situation around. After capturing the stagehands, the party splits again: one group goes to research some topics in the library while the other group stays in the van and interrogates the captives before sending them to jail. This is an example of what's cool about the Monster of the Week system: if the Investigate a Mystery roll succeeded, they would have acquired useful information. Because it failed, they got ambushed instead. This is a challenge, but when they beat it they end up with new information regardless (from the assailants). So **an investigation move always advances the plot**, the result of the roll only determines the way in which it happens.

There's a number of intermediate set pieces here where the party is misled into a trap that explodes which they manage to barely survive from, followed by investigating the warehouse where Jhin had been building his traps (but he already left!), and through this process they learn more about the assassin and his goals. They find out Vex is hiding in the sewers, track her down and meet her.

Eventually the players get to **the finale** where Vex falls into Jhin's trap: she is trapped inside the safe, triggering the mechanism that launches it up high through the roof of the manor. Jhin is ready in a nearby building, with his long-range cannon trained on the safe. At this precise moment, the Snoop completes his climb, reaching the same terrace, and pulls his **"What does that feel like?"** move, distracting the assassin and making them miss the perfect shot. This buys enough time for the Initiate to complete the Eye of Twilight's summoning ritual with Jhin in sight. The ritual immediately sends The Eye of Twilight on top of him, ending the menace. Unfortunately this isn't yet the end: the falling safe is about to crush many members of the party to death in its way down! In a last ditch effort, the Initiate sacrifices himself to push the falling safe away. Truly a heroic end.

### [SPOILERS] Post-Game Feelings ###

It took too long to get the game rolling. Everyone at the table, the game master (me) included, is used to tactical/simulationist games like D&D, and it took us all some time to get the hang of a narrative-focused game like this. That's perfectly fine, but it's worth factoring into your available timeframe.

Still on timeframe: **I underestimated the finale**. I reached the final beat about when I wanted to, but resolving it took way longer than expected. That's somewhat inevitable — the climax is the moment, so everyone wants to flesh it out and build it up as much as possible. A good thing, but it means you move through it that much slower. Keep it in mind.

As a closing note, my advice for prospective game masters: **rely on your players easily and often**. If you don't feel strongly about a roll, or you're stumped on how to handle it in the moment, just ask the player(s) how to resolve it. Unlike systems where the GM is the sole objective adjudicator, here you're the facilitator. You don't need to be objective: hype your players up, or punish them if they spent their Luck. Don't feel you have to adjudicate everything alone, you're all building the story together!
