**D&D 5e ♥ 3-6 players ♥ premade characters (level 5)**

*On the morrow is the anniversary of the university’s founding, so you all go to sleep excited about the coming festivities.*

*Suddenly, you are all awoken by a mental message from the Archdeacon: “The Cake!”. It’s not even daybreak, so it takes you some time to process it but you all come to the same conclusion: something must have happened to the cake that was supposed to highlight tomorrow’s feast!*

*You hurriedly jump out of your pajamas into your work-clothes and head towards the University.*

### Is this for you? ###

This is a D&D 5e adventure meant for:
+ Flexible player count (3 to 6 player characters)
+ Rules-light play / RP
+ New players (players say what they want and DM figures out the how-to’s).
+ Fairy Tale feel, characters are gnomes that live in shrooms, the inciting event is a missing cake (there's still combat).

### Running the Session ###

**Before leaving my home** I (the DM) will have printed the character sheets and filled them in with the details for each of the characters. The spell sheets for each character will have been printed too.

**At the start of the session**, the players are given pre-made characters to choose from, based purely on their descriptions (one liners seen below). Of course if they have questions about their details they can be answered freely but the point is to not overwhelm with (mostly unnecessary) information.
+ **Dean of Divination:** Specialized in Divination magic. Has detection skills and spells as well as some ability to fudge dice in your favor.
+ **Dean of Wilderness:** Specialized in Nature magic. Has outdoor skills and spells and is good at melee combat.
+ **Dean of Defense:** Specialized in Abjuration magic. Has healing skills and spells and is good at protecting itself from enemy magic.
+ **Dean of Offense:** Specialized in Evocation magic. Is good at magic combat and elemental effects.
+ **Dean of Conjuration:** Specialized in Conjuration magic. Has specialized knowledge of the occult, can conjure up new objects and creatures for both every-day use as well as combat.
+ **Dean of Wellness:** Specialized in Muscle magic. Has physical skills and spells and is good at melee combat.
+ **Dean of Lore:** Specialized in the Scriptures. Has great knowledge of many things and useful spells for gathering information.
+ **Dean of Artifice:** Specialized in Runic magic. Has crafting and investigation skills. Their spells are a mixture of utility and explosions and they're good at ranged combat.

At this stage they were also introduced to their remaining faculty members (NPC characters) with similar one-liner descriptions:
+ **Archdean Supreme (NPC):** No one knows if they have any specialization, they are seemingly good at everything!!
+ **Dean of Illusion (NPC):** Specialized in Illusion magic. Is good at playing tricks on others.
+ **Dean of Mushrooms (NPC):** Specialized in Necromantic magic. Has specialized knowledge of the occult, can raise pet mushrooms to perform different tasks.

Once everyone has picked their character, the game can start normally following the script. The script is kept loose, specially towards the end of the adventure; so it might be worth going over it and considering whether you want to keep it that way or tighten some parts in advance.

### Post-Game Feelings (Spoilers!) ###

So this is a one-shot that I ran sometime around 2023, these are some of my thoughts as I remember them.

We ended up having 4 players, with Wellness, Lore and Artifice seeing play. The bits of the game that revolved around **investigation developed pretty well**: there was a bunch of improvisation due to unexpected avenues of investigation but that's to be expected.

The combat was a bigger obstacle than I would have liked and **I recommend removing the first encounter** with the slimes entirely.

**The shamblers** ended up being a much bigger threat than anticipated, but the adventure felt all the more perilous for it; so I would keep them. I would simply **reduce their HP** so the fight doesn't drag for too long after the initial scare.

The finale was fun, and having exorcising the bad men as win con that doesn't necessarily involve fighting anyone felt good. It is specially interesting when you get more than one enemy and the players have limited means to exorcise, so they have to exorcise one of the bosses while keeping the other at bay. The original script is very dry on detail regarding the context of the fight. At the time I settled on: 

*The Illusionist and the Necromancer wanted to eat the cake by themselves, and they go this old abandoned building to do it undisturbed. On the way they find some mushrooms they eat as appetizers, and through the shrooms they get possessed.*

Ideally the players learn about the possession at some point during the fight and come up with ways to handle it that don't involve fighting (or not only). Removing the possession with dispel and then casting protection against evil is a possible way, but there may be others the players come up with. The more roleplay and creativity involved the better!
